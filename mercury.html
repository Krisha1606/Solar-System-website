01 — Overview

Celestial Summary

Mercury is the smallest and innermost planet in the Solar System. Its orbit around the Sun takes only 87.97 Earth days, making it the fastest planet in the Solar System.

Despite being closest to the Sun, Mercury is not the hottest planet — that title belongs to Venus. Mercury lacks a meaningful atmosphere to retain heat, causing extreme temperature swings.

02 — Geology & Structure

Internal Composition

Mercury's surface is heavily cratered and remarkably similar in appearance to Earth's Moon. Ancient volcanic activity shaped vast, smooth plains called 'planitia' that stretch across the planet.

The planet has one of the largest cores relative to its size in the Solar System — its iron core accounts for about 85% of its radius, suggesting it lost much of its outer layers early in solar system history.

Did you know?

"If you could stand on Mercury's surface, the Sun would appear about three times larger in the sky than it does from Earth. The sunrise would be brief and intensely hot."

03 — Atmospheric Data

Planetary Envelope

Instead of a true atmosphere, Mercury has an extremely thin exosphere composed of atoms blasted off its surface by solar wind, meteoroid strikes, and radioactive decay. It provides no thermal insulation whatsoever.

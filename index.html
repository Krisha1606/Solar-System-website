About the Solar System

Our solar system is a vast and dynamic cosmic neighborhood consisting of the Sun—an average star in the Milky Way galaxy—and those objects bound to it by gravity. This includes eight distinct planets, their 200+ known moons, and countless asteroids, comets, and meteoroids.


The system is generally divided into the inner rocky planets (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars) and the outer gas and ice giants (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune). Join us on this journey to deeply explore these magnificent celestial bodies floating eternally in the void.

Celestial Lineup

Mercury

The swiftest planet in our solar system, whipping around the Sun in just 88 days.

Venus

A toxic, super-heated greenhouse world shrouded in thick yellowish clouds of acid.

Earth

Our precious oasis. The only place we know of in the entire universe where life exists.

Mars

A cold desert world covered in iron oxide frost, hiding enormous dead volcanoes.

Jupiter

A gas giant with mass more than twice that of all other planets combined.

Saturn

Adorned with a dazzling, complex system of icy rings; the jewel of the system.

Uranus

An ice giant that bizarrely rotates on its side, making it look like a rolling barrel.

Neptune

The most distant planet. It is dark, extremely cold, and whipped by supersonic winds.

Cosmic Fun Facts

  • A Year on Venus: A day on Venus is actually longer than its year! It takes Venus longer to rotate once on its axis than to complete one orbit around the Sun.
  • Footprints on the Moon: The footprints left by Apollo astronauts will likely stay there for millions of years since there is no atmospheric wind to blow them away.
  • Saturn's Density: Saturn is the only planet in our solar system that is less dense than water. If you had an ocean big enough, Saturn would effortlessly float like an apple!

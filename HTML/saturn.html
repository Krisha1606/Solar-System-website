JUPITER URANUS
01 — Overview

Celestial Summary

Saturn is the sixth planet from the Sun and the second largest. It is the least dense planet in the Solar System — so low in density that it would theoretically float on water.

Saturn's breathtaking ring system makes it the most visually spectacular planet. The rings were first observed by Galileo in 1610, though he couldn't resolve them clearly and described them as 'ears'.

02 — Geology & Structure

Internal Composition

Like Jupiter, Saturn is a hydrogen and helium gas giant with no true solid surface. Its core is likely rocky, surrounded by liquid metallic hydrogen, with cloud layers forming its banded appearance.

Saturn's hexagonal north polar storm, a persistent six-sided jet stream pattern, is one of the Solar System's most bizarre structures. Each side of the hexagon is longer than Earth's diameter.

Did you know?

"Saturn has 146 known moons — the most of any planet. Its largest moon, Titan, is bigger than Mercury and has a thick nitrogen atmosphere with rivers and lakes of liquid methane on its surface."

03 — Atmospheric Data

Planetary Envelope

Saturn's atmosphere is similar to Jupiter's but its clouds are higher, thinner, and much more muted in color. Winds at the equator can reach 1,800 km/h — far faster than any wind ever recorded on Earth.

Mission Briefing

NASA's Cassini spacecraft orbited Saturn from 2004 to 2017, revolutionizing our understanding of the ringed planet. It plunged into Saturn's atmosphere at the end of its mission to protect Titan and Enceladus from contamination.

01 — Overview

Celestial Summary

Jupiter is the fifth planet from the Sun and by far the largest in the Solar System — more than twice as massive as all the other planets combined. It is a gas giant with no solid surface.

Jupiter's immense gravity has profoundly shaped the Solar System. It acts as a giant 'vacuum cleaner,' attracting and absorbing comets and asteroids that might otherwise strike the inner planets.

02 — Geology & Structure

Internal Composition

Jupiter has no solid surface. Moving inward, gas becomes liquid, and deeper still, hydrogen is compressed into a metallic liquid state that conducts electricity, generating Jupiter's powerful magnetic field.

The Great Red Spot — an iconic, persistent high-pressure storm larger than Earth — has been observed for over 350 years. Recent data shows it is slowly shrinking but remains one of the most powerful storms in the Solar System.

Did you know?

"Jupiter has 95 known moons — more than any other planet. Four of them (Io, Europa, Ganymede, and Callisto) were discovered by Galileo in 1610 and are large enough to be seen with binoculars."

03 — Atmospheric Data

Planetary Envelope

Jupiter's atmosphere is made up predominantly of hydrogen and helium. The visible cloud bands are driven by fierce jet streams. The vivid colors come from compounds like ammonia ice, sulfur, and phosphine.

Mission Briefing

NASA's Juno spacecraft has been orbiting Jupiter since 2016, studying its deep atmosphere, magnetic field, and auroras. The Europa Clipper mission, launching in 2024, will explore Europa's potentially habitable ocean.

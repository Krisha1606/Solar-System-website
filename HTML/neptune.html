URANUS MERCURY
01 — Overview

Celestial Summary

Neptune is the eighth and farthest known planet from the Sun. It is the smallest of the ice giants, but is denser and more massive than Uranus, resulting in slightly stronger surface gravity.

Neptune was the first planet predicted mathematically before it was visually observed. In 1846, it was discovered by astronomers following unexplained perturbations in Uranus's orbit.

02 — Geology & Structure

Internal Composition

Neptune's internal structure is similar to Uranus's: a rocky silicate core surrounded by a slushy mantle of water, ammonia, and methane ices. Its striking blue color comes from methane in the atmosphere.

Neptune's largest moon, Triton, orbits the planet backwards and is thought to be a captured Kuiper Belt Object. It has geysers that erupt nitrogen gas and is slowly spiraling inward — destined to be torn apart by Neptune's gravity.

Did you know?

"A year on Neptune lasts 165 Earth years. Neptune completed its very first orbit since its discovery in 1846 only in July 2011 — just over a decade ago. No human generation has ever observed a full Neptunian year."

03 — Atmospheric Data

Planetary Envelope

Neptune has the strongest sustained winds in the Solar System, with gusts reaching up to 2,100 km/h. A massive storm system called the Great Dark Spot was observed by Voyager 2 in 1989, though it had disappeared by 1994.

01 — Overview

Celestial Summary

Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun — a cold, dusty desert world with a thin atmosphere. Yet it harbors the most dramatic geology in the Solar System.

Mars has intrigued scientists for centuries. Evidence of ancient river valleys, lake beds, and minerals that form in water strongly suggest Mars was once a warmer, wetter world capable of supporting life.

02 — Geology & Structure

Internal Composition

Mars is home to Olympus Mons, the tallest volcano in the Solar System at 21.9 km high — nearly three times the height of Everest. Valles Marineris, a canyon system, stretches over 4,000 km.

Iron oxide (rust) dust covering the Martian surface gives the planet its iconic red color. This fine dust is also responsible for global dust storms that can engulf the entire planet for months.

Did you know?

"Scientists have found compelling evidence of ancient liquid water on Mars — including dried riverbeds, lake deposits, and water-formed minerals — raising the possibility that Mars once hosted microbial life."

03 — Atmospheric Data

Planetary Envelope

Mars has a very thin atmosphere composed mostly of carbon dioxide. It provides little insulation or protection from solar radiation, and atmospheric pressure at the surface is less than 1% of Earth's.

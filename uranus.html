01 — Overview

Celestial Summary

Uranus is the seventh planet from the Sun and the third largest by diameter. It is classified as an ice giant, composed mainly of 'icy' materials — water, methane, and ammonia — rather than gas.

Uranus is unique in that its axis of rotation is tilted at 98 degrees, meaning it essentially rolls around the Sun on its side. This extreme tilt causes unusual seasonal cycles lasting 21 Earth years each.

02 — Geology & Structure

Internal Composition

Uranus has a complex, multi-layered internal structure. A rocky silicate core sits at the center, surrounded by a slushy mantle of water, methane, and ammonia ices under extreme pressure.

Uranus has 13 known rings, first discovered in 1977. The rings are dark, narrow, and composed of relatively large particles ranging from centimeters to several meters in size.

Did you know?

"Uranus is the only planet to rotate with its axis tilted almost perpendicular to its orbit. Scientists believe a massive collision with an Earth-sized object billions of years ago knocked it onto its side."

03 — Atmospheric Data

Planetary Envelope

Uranus has the coldest planetary atmosphere in the Solar System, reaching a minimum temperature of −224°C. Methane gas in its upper atmosphere absorbs red light, giving Uranus its distinctive pale blue-green color.

Mission Briefing

Voyager 2 is the only spacecraft to have visited Uranus, flying past in 1986. A Uranus Orbiter and Probe mission was identified as the top priority for planetary science in NASA's 2023–2032 Planetary Science Decadal Survey.
